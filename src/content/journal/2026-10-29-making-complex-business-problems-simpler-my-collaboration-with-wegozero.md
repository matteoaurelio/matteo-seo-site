One of the things I enjoy most about working independently is the opportunity to enter industries I know relatively little about, understand how they operate, and find practical ways to contribute.

My recent collaboration with Stefanie Behrendt, CEO of [WeGoZero](https://www.wegozero.co/), an Amsterdam-based circular economy consultancy, is a good example of this.

WeGoZero helps businesses understand what happens to their waste, identify opportunities to reduce costs, and find ways to reuse materials that would otherwise be discarded. Its work combines environmental expertise with operational and financial considerations.

For me, this collaboration has opened up a new area of learning while allowing me to apply skills I have developed across technology, data analytics and business operations.

## Understanding the business before building anything

My professional background has largely involved digital products, payment systems, analytics and improving online customer experiences.

Working with WeGoZero has introduced me to the economics of waste management.

A **waste audit**, for example, involves examining the types and quantities of materials a business discards, how they are handled, and whether they could be reduced, reused or recovered.

I've always worked with data, but it was not until I started digging into this industry that data, if used correctly, **has the potential to transform the entire waste management industry.** 

How much information is actually hidden inside ordinary operational processes?

WeGoZero has [publicly described](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-1567-tonnes-waste-data-taught-us-zerowasteconsultancy-ppp0f) how discrepancies between waste records and actual disposal practices can lead businesses to underestimate and de-prioritize the real hidden value of their waste streams.  

In agriculture, banking and tech companies it's the same: we are producing ENORMOUS amounts of data and even with AI, the majority of organizations are under-acting on that information. The gap is not easy to close. 

Data and information is generated by terabytes while our speed is still on the megabyte level. 

## Where I contributed and I'm still looking forward to more

My contribution to WeGoZero has focused on its digital presence, particularly how to communicate a specialised B2B service more clearly and make the website easier to develop and maintain.

Environmental consulting involves concepts that may be unfamiliar to prospective customers. Circularity, waste diversion, material recovery and regulatory compliance all have specific meanings, but business clients ultimately need to understand how these concepts affect their operations.

This creates an interesting challenge.

How do you take a technically sophisticated service and explain it in a way that a hotel manager, construction company or business owner can understand quickly?

I've been approaching the website work from both a commercial and technical perspective: understanding what prospective customers need to know, organizing information around their questions, and translating those decisions into a usable digital experience.

On the technical side, I've been working with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and WordPress integration, with an emphasis on lightweight code, maintainability and keeping future changes manageable.

The new SEO professionals should be able to combine technical know-how with business insights to drive value for their organization, and that's what I tried to do at WeGoZero.

There is also a useful discipline in working within an existing technology environment. The best solution has to consider what a business can realistically maintain after the initial development work is complete.

## Learning to connect environmental and economic value

One area I want to explore further is how companies make investment decisions around sustainability.

Environmental benefits matter, but businesses also face operational constraints, competing priorities and financial pressures.

For example, improving how materials are separated and recovered may reduce disposal costs, create opportunities to sell reusable materials, or improve purchasing practices. Understanding these mechanisms makes the discussion more concrete.

This is where I see considerable potential in WeGoZero's work. Its environmental knowledge can help organizations identify opportunities that are easy to overlook in everyday operations.

From my perspective, the interesting challenge is how to make those opportunities understandable and actionable through better communication and digital tools.

A clearer website is one small part of that process. Over time, better digital experiences and measurement could also help connect more interested businesses with the expertise they need.

## What this means for my development as an independent consultant

Independence has changed how I think about professional growth.

When working inside a larger organization, responsibilities are often defined by a particular product, department or technical function. As an independent consultant, I have more opportunities to understand an entire business, from its services and customers to the practical difficulties of delivering its work.

I also enjoy my conversations and the trust developed with Stefanie; it is also these types of conversations that spark my interest and curiosity to dig deeper and propose new solutions outside the box. 

With WeGoZero, I bring experience in technology, analytics and digital business development. In return, I am gaining a better understanding of circular business models, resource management and the economic challenges surrounding sustainability.

It is also helping me strengthen something I consider fundamental to my work: the ability to move from a business problem to a practical implementation without introducing unnecessary complexity.

There is still work to develop, test and improve in this collaboration. I value the opportunity to learn through that process and contribute alongside people with expertise different from my own.

Ultimately, this is the direction I want to continue developing professionally: working independently with organizations solving real problems, understanding their industries in depth, and applying technology and commercial thinking where they can make a useful contribution.
